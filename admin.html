Foundations · Decision Brief · 9.5-Year Archive

A decade of freight says yes — on five loops, in a specific order.

Every conclusion below is computed from RJS's complete McLeod history — 192,699 orders, February 2017 through August 2026 — then stress-tested in a Monte Carlo simulator. No market data, no estimates from decks.

Recommendation: launch on the three Tier-1 loops — Richburg↔Huntsville, Wadsworth↔Richburg, and Pompano↔Plant City — with two policy moves and no new technology: commit each shipper to one weekday (the −4% buys the day) and pay carriers up to 3 days of layover ($150/day) to hold for the rebound. The simulator prices those two moves at roughly 2–3× today's loop completion. Everything else waits for a second broker's freight.

At a 3% net margin, loop economics are not an optimization — they are a rescue. The repriced book adds +$100 of margin per rotation with every shipper still paying at or below today's rate (funded by the carrier concession, which the truck accepts because it earns +78–90% per mile driven vs deadheading). 2026 is the urgency: volume is at 4× the historical pace while after-commission margin fell to 5.15% — the worst since startup — and loss trucks run at 7.6× the decade average.

Conclusions

  1. The loops are structural, not seasonal. Five corridors ran both directions for 6–10 consecutive years through the 2018 boom, COVID, the 2021–22 spike and the 2023–24 recession (heat matrix above). Monroe↔Monaca has never missed a year since Feb 2017.
  2. The problem is real and quantified. 93.2% of recent loads have no reciprocal in the book → ~40.4M empty miles/yr ≈ $50.5M of empty running at $1.25/mi (measured Jun–Aug 2026 window, annualized ×5.69).
  3. Two policy moves, no new tech, 2–3× completion. Committed weekday + up to 3 days paid layover lifts the Tier-1 loops from 15–17 to 38–44 of every 100 (40k-run MC per scenario). Trucks are never the constraint (3× pool = +0%).
  4. Everyone gets paid from the destroyed empty mile. Repriced: shipper at or below today, truck +78–90% per mile driven, RJS +$100/rotation ($72K/yr across 9 loops vs $5.4K under flat pricing).
  5. At ~3% net margin this is a rescue, not an optimization. 16.0% gross − 57% commissions − overhead ≈ 3% net (partners confirm). One recovered margin point ≈ a third of net profit — and 2026 is diluting hardest while volume quadruples.
  6. The ceiling needs other brokers. Imbalanced lanes (Plant City→Oxford 739/164, Tifton set) and the 443 orphan lanes are unfixable inside one book at any price; cross-broker fill is their only lever (+26% fee in simulation). Fee on the five launch loops is ~$5–8K/yr — the marketplace case is the network, proven here.
  7. Act before the loops die. Pompano↔Salisbury ran balanced for 7 years (423/352) and went to zero in 2026. Richburg↔Tulsa (0.94 balance) died the same way. Bulletproof ≠ immortal.

The plan

  1. Week 1 — Richburg↔Huntsville (ATI Allvac both legs, 397 mi/leg flatbed). Shipper: $2,490 → $2,390/rotation (−4%), never above today. Carrier: $1,967 gross − $39 fee = $1,928 net for 824 loaded mi = $2.34/mi — 1.79× the one-way rate (today: $1,080 then 424 mi home empty at $1.31/mi driven). RJS margin $323 → $423 (+$100/rotation). Foundations 2% = $39, paid to carrier on confirmation. Layover budget: $150/day × ≤2 days = $300 max, shipper-paid when their AM dock window causes the wait. Sim: 17→44 of 100.
  2. Week 2 — Wadsworth↔Richburg (0.91 balance, 9 straight years, 501 mi/leg). Shipper $2,852 → $2,740. Carrier $2,130 gross − $43 = $2,087 net = $2.02/mi, 1.88× one-way. RJS $510 → $610. Commit Thu out / Mon back; wait bridges the weekend — 50/50 layover split ($150/day), and the 34-hour HOS restart doubles as the wait, so the truck loses nothing.
  3. Week 3 — Pompano↔Plant City (intra-FL reefer, 238 mi/leg, books same-day). Price at lane benchmark −4%; carrier floor ≥1.60× one-way $/mi (repricing rule, not a quote — this pair has no 9-week model yet). No layover line: legs are same-day; this is just-in-time matching at delivery, ±90-min pickup windows. Sim: 16→42.
  4. Month 2 — the anchors. Monroe↔Monaca (10-of-10 years): shipper $3,772, carrier $3,210 − $64 = $3,146 net (concession 7.4%), RJS $462 → $562. Plus South Boston↔Richburg (205 mi/leg — margin curve says short loops first: sub-250mi lanes run 19–22.5% vs 11.5% long-haul).
  5. Month 3+ — the imbalanced reefer set (Plant City→Oxford 739/164, Tifton, Immokalee): every in-house lever ≈ +0–5 per 100; these justify broker #2. Reactivation target: Pompano↔Salisbury (7 years, 423/352, died 2026).
  6. Standing rules across all loops: fee 2% of carrier pay (vs 3.5–4.5% factoring), paid on trip confirmation; layover $150/day capped at 3 days ($450); free alternative first: a pull-forward request to the shipper (accepted ~50% in sim = +52% fee); detention $65/hr after 2h, shipper-paid; no-show = release + $250 + strike; every rotation must satisfy the +$100 RJS-uplift floor before tender.
Honest limits. Loop completion rates are simulated (40k runs/scenario) from measured cadence, weekday concentration, booking lead time, and miles — carrier acceptance is still an assumed input. Metro cells are ~40mi: "Monaca" is the Monessen/Pittsburgh steel cell, "Wadsworth" the Wooster cell, "Oxford" the Henderson NC cell. Fee projections use blended $2.60/mi flatbed pay. The 2% marketplace fee on these five loops is beer money (~$5–8K/yr) — the recommendation is justified by RJS margin recovery and proof-of-concept for the broker network, not by fee revenue.
